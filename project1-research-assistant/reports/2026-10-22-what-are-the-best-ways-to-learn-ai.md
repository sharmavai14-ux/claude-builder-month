# Best Ways to Learn AI (October 2026)

## Short answer
Start with the basic ideas of AI, then get good at one or two AI tools, and then build small real projects. That is the advice of a 2026 learning guide on the DeepLearning.AI community forum. My search tool hit its limit after one search, so this report is based on only that one source. Treat it as a starting point, not a full review.

## Details
- **Learn the basics before the tools.** The guide says to start by understanding what AI is and how it differs from machine learning and deep learning. It also says to learn how large language models (LLMs, the kind of system behind chatbots) work, the basics of neural networks, and the common words people use.
- **You don't need hard math to begin.** According to the guide, at first you should focus on how AI systems solve problems and what they're good and bad at. Advanced math isn't needed early on.
- **Get good at one or two tools before trying lots.** The guide says not to jump between dozens of AI tools. Learn one or two major ones well, using them to summarize documents, write content, brainstorm, automate repeated tasks and help with coding. After that, other tools are easier to pick up.
- **Build real projects.** The guide calls solving real problems "the fastest way to learn AI." One example it gives is building your own personal AI assistant.
- **What I couldn't check.** I wasn't able to confirm details about well-known courses, such as Andrew Ng's courses on Coursera or DeepLearning.AI, fast.ai's Practical Deep Learning, Google's Machine Learning Crash Course, or Elements of AI. These are widely recommended, but you should check their current content and prices yourself.
- **If you want to build AI, not just use it.** From general knowledge and not from my search: people who want to make AI systems usually also learn Python and some basic statistics and linear algebra (the math of tables of numbers) along the way. This is a common path, but my source didn't cover it.
