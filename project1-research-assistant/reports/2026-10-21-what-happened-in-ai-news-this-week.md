# AI news for the week ending about 16–20 October 2026

## Short answer
I could only check a few sources before my search tool hit its usage limit. Almost everything I found is from around Friday, 16 October, so I couldn't confirm what has happened since Monday, 19 October. The main stories from that time: several new AI models came out (from Meta, NVIDIA, DeepSeek and Microsoft), more people worried about AI being used for hacking, and OpenAI faced a lawsuit from eight US states.

## Details
- **New models:** NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL. It's an open model, which means anyone can download it. It works with text, images and video. Meta also released an open model that The Neuron says can run on two Mac Studio computers. The site llm-stats.com lists a new DeepSeek model, V4.1-Flash, dated 16 October.
- **Microsoft's big model:** A daily summary posted on X says Microsoft has finished the first stage of training a very large model called MAI-Base-2. The post says Microsoft found it beat every open model it tested. That stage is only the first part of training. More work is still needed before the model is ready, and these claims come from Microsoft's own tests, not outside checks.
- **AI finding software flaws:** The Neuron reported that Bank of America hired 1,000 contractors to fix security flaws that Anthropic's "Mythos" model found. On the same topic, 420 researchers signed an open letter asking companies to keep AI models with strong hacking skills cut off from the internet while they are first being tested.
- **OpenAI in court and in safety news:** Eight US states sued OpenAI over its claims about how safe ChatGPT is. The Neuron also covered an OpenAI safety report on GPT-6. In that report, the model nearly aced one safety test but kept failing tests that copied real work settings. Separately, ChatGPT voice got new voices in six languages.
- **Fake videos in court:** Speakers told a US federal judges' panel that AI-made audio and video are getting very hard to tell apart from the real thing. They said courts shouldn't count on one tool being able to spot every fake.
- **Earlier in October (around 9 October):** MarketingProfs reported that OpenAI planned to test image ads inside ChatGPT for some free US users. It also covered rising worries that huge spending on AI data centers may not pay off fast enough.

**A caution:** The Neuron's daily newsletter and an X post are the sources for most of this, not official company announcements. For news from 19 October onward, check a major news site or the companies' own blogs.
